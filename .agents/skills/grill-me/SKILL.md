# Grill Me

Conduct a relentless, one-question-at-a-time interview to pressure-test whatever
plan, design, or decision is currently on the table. The goal isn't to be
hostile — it's to surface the weak points the user hasn't stress-tested yet,
before reality does it for them.

## Setup

1. Identify the subject: whatever plan/design/decision is already in the
   conversation. If nothing concrete is in scope, ask the user what to grill.
2. Briefly restate the subject in one or two sentences so the user can correct
   your understanding before the interview starts.

## Rules of the interview

- Ask **one question at a time**. Wait for the answer before asking the next.
- Every question must be earned by something the user actually said — no
  generic checklists recited at them.
- Go after the highest-risk weak point first: the assumption that, if wrong,
  breaks the most.
- Push on:
  - **Assumptions** presented as facts — what's the evidence?
  - **Edge cases / failure modes** — what happens when the happy path doesn't hold?
  - **Scope and cost** — what's being underestimated or left unstated?
  - **Alternatives** — what else was considered, and why was it rejected?
  - **Reversibility** — how bad is it if this is wrong, and can it be undone?
  - **Vague or hand-wavy answers** — don't let "it should be fine" or "we'll
    figure it out later" pass. Follow up until you get a specific answer or an
    honest "I don't know."
- If an answer reveals a new weak point, follow it before moving to the next
  planned question — depth over coverage.
- Stay respectful and direct. No performative toughness, no gotchas for their
  own sake — every question should be one the user is genuinely better off
  having answered.
- Keep going until either the plan's real weak points have been surfaced and
  addressed, or the user asks to stop.

## Closing

When the interview winds down, summarize:
- What held up under questioning.
- What changed or should change as a result.
- Any open risks the user is knowingly accepting rather than solving now.
